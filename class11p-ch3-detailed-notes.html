1. Introduction to Vectors

Physical quantities are classified into two main types based on direction properties:

  • Scalars: Quantities that have magnitude only and no direction (e.g., mass, distance, speed, time, temperature). They follow ordinary laws of algebra.
  • Vectors: Quantities that have both magnitude and a specific direction, and obey the special laws of vector addition (e.g., displacement, velocity, acceleration, force).

2. Representation of a Vector

Symbolic Representation:

A vector is represented graphically by a directed line segment (an arrow). The length represents magnitude, and the arrowhead points in the direction. Denoted as $\vec{A}$ or $\mathbf{A}$. Magnitude is written as $|\vec{A}|$ or $A$.

Equation Representation:

In Cartesian coordinates using orthogonal unit vectors $\hat{i}$, $\hat{j}$, $\hat{k}$ along X, Y, and Z axes:

$$\vec{A} = A_x\hat{i} + A_y\hat{j} + A_z\hat{k}$$

Magnitude formula:

$$|\vec{A}| = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2 + A_z^2}$$

3. Various Types of Vectors

  • Null Vector (Zero Vector): A vector with zero magnitude ($\vec{0}$) and an arbitrary direction. (e.g., velocity of a stationary object).
  • Unit Vector: A vector of magnitude 1 used exclusively to specify direction ($\hat{A} = \frac{\vec{A}}{|\vec{A}|}$).
  • Position Vector: Specifies the position of a point relative to the origin: $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$.
  • Displacement Vector: Represents change in position: $\Delta\vec{r} = \vec{r_2} - \vec{r_1}$.
  • Equal Vectors: Vectors having identical magnitudes and directions.
  • Like & Unlike Vectors: Like vectors point in the same direction; unlike vectors point in opposite directions.
  • Negative of a Vector: Same magnitude but opposite direction ($-\vec{A}$).
  • Parallel & Collinear Vectors: Parallel vectors act along parallel lines ($0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$); collinear vectors act along the same line.
  • Coplanar Vectors: Vectors lying in the same plane.
  • Coinitial Vectors: Vectors sharing the exact same starting point.

4. Properties of Vectors

  • Commutative Law: $\vec{A} + \vec{B} = \vec{B} + \vec{A}$
  • Associative Law: $(\vec{A} + \vec{B}) + \vec{C} = \vec{A} + (\vec{B} + \vec{C})$
  • Distributive Law: $m(\vec{A} + \vec{B}) = m\vec{A} + m\vec{B}$ (where $m$ is a scalar)

5. Addition of Vectors & Parallelogram Law (Derivation)

Parallelogram Law Statement: If two vectors acting simultaneously at a point are represented in magnitude and direction by two adjacent sides of a parallelogram, their resultant is represented by the diagonal passing through that point.

Step-by-Step Derivation of Resultant Magnitude & Direction:

1. Let two vectors $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ be represented by sides $OP$ and $OQ$ of a parallelogram, inclined at an angle $\theta$.

2. Extend line $OP$ and draw a perpendicular $QN$ from point $Q$ onto $OP$ produced, meeting at point $N$.

3. In right-angled triangle $\triangle ONQ$:

$$OQ^2 = ON^2 + QN^2$$

4. Since $ON = OP + PN$, substitute this:

$$R^2 = (OP + PN)^2 + QN^2 \quad \text{--- (Equation 1)}$$

5. In right-angled triangle $\triangle QPN$, let $\angle QPN = \theta$:

$$PN = B\cos\theta \quad \text{and} \quad QN = B\sin\theta$$

6. Substitute $OP = A$, $PN$, and $QN$ into Equation 1:

$$R^2 = (A + B\cos\theta)^2 + (B\sin\theta)^2$$
$$R^2 = A^2 + B^2\cos^2\theta + 2AB\cos\theta + B^2\sin^2\theta$$
$$R^2 = A^2 + B^2(\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) + 2AB\cos\theta$$

Since $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$, the final magnitude formula is:

$$R = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2 + 2AB\cos\theta}$$

Direction ($\alpha$ with vector $\vec{A}$):

From right-angled triangle $\triangle ONQ$, $\tan\alpha = \frac{QN}{ON} = \frac{QN}{OP + PN}$

$$\tan\alpha = \frac{B\sin\theta}{A + B\cos\theta}$$

6. Subtraction of Vectors

Vector subtraction is the addition of a negative vector: $\vec{A} - \vec{B} = \vec{A} + (-\vec{B})$.

$$R_{sub} = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2 - 2AB\cos\theta}$$

7. Resolution of Vectors

Splitting a vector $\vec{A}$ into rectangular components along orthogonal axes (making angle $\theta$ with X-axis):

$$A_x = A\cos\theta, \quad A_y = A\sin\theta \implies \vec{A} = A_x\hat{i} + A_y\hat{j}$$

8. Scalar (Dot) and Vector (Cross) Products

A. Scalar (Dot) Product:

$$\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = AB\cos\theta = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z$$

Example: Work done $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = Fs\cos\theta$.

B. Vector (Cross) Product:

$$\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = (AB\sin\theta)\hat{n} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ A_x & A_y & A_z \\ B_x & B_y & B_z \end{vmatrix}$$

Example: Torque $\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}$.

9. Sine Law and Cosine Law (Derivations)

A. Cosine Law Derivation

Consider any triangle with side vectors $\vec{a}$, $\vec{b}$, and $\vec{c}$ such that $\vec{a} + \vec{b} = \vec{c}$. Taking the dot product of a vector with itself ($c^2 = \vec{c} \cdot \vec{c}$):

$$c^2 = (\vec{a} + \vec{b}) \cdot (\vec{a} + \vec{b}) = a^2 + b^2 + 2\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b}$$

Since $\vec{a}\cdot\vec{b} = ab\cos(180^\circ - C) = -ab\cos C$:

$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$$

B. Sine Law Derivation

Consider a triangle with sides $a, b, c$ and angles $A, B, C$. The area of the triangle can be expressed in three ways using vector cross-product magnitudes:

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C = \frac{1}{2}bc\sin A = \frac{1}{2}ac\sin B$$

Dividing throughout by $\frac{1}{2}abc$ yields the Sine Law:

$$\frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c}$$
