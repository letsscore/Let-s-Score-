1. Introduction to Vectors (NCERT Baseline)

In physics, quantities are classified based on their direction properties:

  • Scalars: Quantities with magnitude only (e.g., mass, length, time, speed, work). They follow ordinary rules of algebra.
  • Vectors: Quantities possessing both magnitude and a specific direction, and which combine according to the vector addition laws (e.g., displacement, velocity, acceleration, force).

Example:

A car traveling at $60\text{ km/h}$ is speed (scalar), but traveling $60\text{ km/h}$ towards the North is velocity (vector).

2. Representation of a Vector

Symbolic Representation:

Represented graphically by an arrow-headed line segment where length corresponds to magnitude and arrow indicates direction. Written as $\vec{A}$ or $\mathbf{A}$. Magnitude is denoted by $|\vec{A}|$ or $A$.

Equation Representation:

In Cartesian coordinates using orthogonal unit vectors $\hat{i}$, $\hat{j}$, $\hat{k}$:

$$\vec{A} = A_x\hat{i} + A_y\hat{j} + A_z\hat{k}$$

Magnitude formula:

$$|\vec{A}| = \sqrt{A_x^2 + A_y^2 + A_z^2}$$

3. Various Types of Vectors

  • Null Vector (Zero Vector): Magnitude is zero ($\vec{0}$); arbitrary direction. Example: Velocity of a stationary particle.
  • Unit Vector: Magnitude equals 1, used exclusively to designate direction. $\hat{A} = \frac{\vec{A}}{|\vec{A}|}$.
  • Position Vector: Locates an object relative to the coordinate origin: $\vec{r} = x\hat{i} + y\hat{j} + z\hat{k}$.
  • Displacement Vector: Change in position vector from initial to final point: $\Delta\vec{r} = \vec{r_2} - \vec{r_1}$.
  • Equal Vectors: Equal magnitudes and identical directions.
  • Unequal Vectors: Varying magnitudes or different directions.
  • Like Vectors: Vectors pointing in the same direction irrespective of magnitude.
  • Unlike Vectors: Vectors pointing in opposite directions.
  • Negative of a Vector: Equal magnitude but directly opposite orientation ($-\vec{A}$).
  • Parallel Vectors: Lines of action are parallel ($0^\circ$ angle).
  • Coplanar Vectors: Vectors lying on the same plane.
  • Collinear Vectors: Vectors acting along the same single line.
  • Coinitial Vectors: Vectors sharing a common starting origin point.

4. Properties of Vectors

1. Commutative Law:

$$\vec{A} + \vec{B} = \vec{B} + \vec{A}$$

2. Associative Law:

$$(\vec{A} + \vec{B}) + \vec{C} = \vec{A} + (\vec{B} + \vec{C})$$

3. Distributive Law:

$$m(\vec{A} + \vec{B}) = m\vec{A} + m\vec{B}$$

5. Addition of Vectors & Laws of Vector Addition

A. Triangle Law

If two vectors are represented by two continuous sides of a triangle in order, their sum (resultant) is represented by the third side taken in opposite order.

B. Parallelogram Law (With Derivation)

If two vectors $\vec{A}$ and $\vec{B}$ form adjacent sides of a parallelogram meeting at angle $\theta$, the diagonal represents the magnitude of the resultant $\vec{R}$:

$$R = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2 + 2AB\cos\theta}$$

Direction ($\alpha$) with vector $\vec{A}$:

$$\tan\alpha = \frac{B\sin\theta}{A + B\cos\theta}$$

Numerical Example:

Find the resultant of two vectors of magnitude $3\text{ units}$ and $4\text{ units}$ acting at an angle of $90^\circ$.

Solution: $R = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2 + 2(3)(4)\cos 90^\circ} = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 0} = \sqrt{25} = 5\text{ units}$.

6. Subtraction of Vectors

Defined as adding the negative vector: $\vec{A} - \vec{B} = \vec{A} + (-\vec{B})$.

$$R_{sub} = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2 - 2AB\cos\theta}$$

7. Resolution of Vectors

Splitting vector $\vec{A}$ into rectangular components along axes where it makes angle $\theta$ with the X-axis:

$$\vec{A} = A_x\hat{i} + A_y\hat{j} \quad \text{where } A_x = A\cos\theta, A_y = A\sin\theta$$

8. Scalar (Dot) and Vector (Cross) Product

A. Scalar Product

$$\vec{A} \cdot \vec{B} = AB\cos\theta = A_x B_x + A_y B_y + A_z B_z$$

Example:

Work $W = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{s} = Fs\cos\theta$.

B. Vector Product

$$\vec{A} \times \vec{B} = (AB\sin\theta)\hat{n} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{i} & \hat{j} & \hat{k} \\ A_x & A_y & A_z \\ B_x & B_y & B_z \end{vmatrix}$$

Example:

Torque $\vec{\tau} = \vec{r} \times \vec{F}$.

9. Sine Law and Cosine Law in Vectors

A. Cosine Law (Magnitude Form)

Derived directly from vector triangle properties, relating side lengths ($a, b, c$):

$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$$

B. Sine Law

Relates the ratio of side lengths to the sine of their opposite angles:

$$\frac{\sin A}{a} = \frac{\sin B}{b} = \frac{\sin C}{c}$$

Numerical Example:

In a triangle vector loop, if side $a = 7$, side $b = 10$, and included angle $C = 60^\circ$, find side $c$.

Solution:
$c^2 = 7^2 + 10^2 - 2(7)(10)\cos 60^\circ$
$c^2 = 49 + 100 - 140(0.5) = 149 - 70 = 79$
$c = \sqrt{79} \approx 8.88\text{ units}$.
